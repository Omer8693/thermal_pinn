\section*{L-Shape Adaptive-$k$ Schedules (Per-Seed)}
\label{app:lshape_kschedules}

Table~\ref{tab:lshape_k_schedules} shows the $k$-schedule used by each of the
ten seeds on the L-Shape domain.
On the Square, Circle, and Flower domains all ten seeds converged to the
identical schedule for every architecture.
The L-Shape is the only domain where schedules vary.

The reason is the reentrant corner.
The 270° corner creates a concentrated, localised temperature gradient at the
start of the quench.
The first-window MAE on L-Shape is higher and more sensitive to weight
initialisation than on smooth geometries.
For Bayesian/TPE, nine seeds produce a first-window MAE that falls below the
$\tau_\mathrm{up}=2.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ promotion threshold at window 5,
but seed~9 reaches this threshold one window later.
The result is a one-window shift in the schedule (9 windows vs 10 windows
total).
For NSGA-II the effect is larger: the compact $3{\times}153$ tanh network has
higher first-window error on L-Shape, and three seeds promote one window
earlier than the other seven.
NSGA-III is more stable; all ten seeds converge to the same schedule.
This seed-dependent behaviour explains why the FEM saving for L-Shape
NSGA-II varies between 83\% and 85\% across seeds.

\chapter{Ablation Study}
\label{app:ablation}

We conducted two targeted ablation experiments on the Thermal Fin benchmark
with the Bayesian/TPE architecture, using three seeds each.

\section{Fourier Feature Ablation}
\label{sec:app_fourier}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Fourier feature ablation --- Thermal Fin, Bayesian/TPE.
Mean-window MAE ($^\circ\mathrm{C}$), $\bar{x}\pm\sigma$ across 3 seeds.
$\Delta$ = Fourier $-$ plain.}
\label{tab:fourier_ablation}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Configuration & $k{=}1$ & $k{=}2$ & $k{=}3$ & $k{=}4$ & $k{=}5$ \\
\midrule
Plain MLP  & $9.45\pm0.12$ & $10.41\pm0.86$ & $10.73\pm0.32$ & $13.78\pm1.18$ & $14.29\pm0.64$ \\
Fourier ON & $5.07\pm0.05$ & $6.87\pm0.11$  & $8.84\pm0.11$  & $10.81\pm0.24$ & $12.50\pm0.29$ \\
\midrule
$\Delta$   & $-4.38$ & $-3.55$ & $-1.89$ & $-2.97$ & $-1.79$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Fourier features reduce MAE by $1.8$--$4.4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ across all skip
factors and also reduce seed variance.
The improvement is specific to Bayesian/TPE; applying the same embedding to
NSGA-II and NSGA-III produced substantially higher errors because the
128-dimensional Fourier input overwhelms their three-layer tanh capacity.

\section{Endpoint Supervision Ablation}
\label{sec:app_endpoint}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Endpoint supervision ablation --- Thermal Fin, Bayesian/TPE, plain MLP.
Mean-window MAE ($^\circ\mathrm{C}$).}
\label{tab:endpoint_ablation}

\begin{tabular}{lccccc}

Removing endpoint supervision causes catastrophic degradation
($+49$--$77\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$), confirming that the FEM anchor field at the
end of each window is an essential training signal.
